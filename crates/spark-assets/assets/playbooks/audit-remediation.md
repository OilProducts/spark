---
title: Audit remediation
description: Triage verified audit findings into bounded fix batches, implement and independently review each batch in isolated worktrees, and deliver within the mission's authority.
---

Take a verified codebase audit (or an explicit slice of one) from backlog to verified, delivered fixes. The mission's objective names the audit source, scope boundaries, constraints, and delivery authority.

1. Read and triage: read the audit report and backlog in full, then check each in-scope finding against current `HEAD`. Drop findings already fixed or out of scope, separate items blocked on a product or policy decision from actionable fixes, and group closely related actionable items into small, coherent batches (highest severity first) rather than one sprawling pass.
2. Design only where warranted: launch `software-development/design-change` only for items that reshape a trust boundary, wire contract, or cross-cutting architecture. For self-contained defects with clear root causes and acceptance criteria, go straight to implementation.
3. Implement in bounded batches: for each batch, launch `software-development/implement-change` with the batch's findings, target scope, acceptance criteria, constraints, and validation command. Keep all edits in isolated worktrees (chaining batches with `context.request.base_ref` on one mission branch or delivering batch by batch as appropriate); never edit the main checkout directly.
4. Independently verify each batch: launch `software-development/review-change` with the resulting branch or commit as `context.request.source_ref`. Check the diff, real callers/boundaries, and validation evidence yourself—confirming each finding's concrete failure scenario is fixed without regressions. Fix material defects before proceeding.
5. Deliver within the mission's authority: when merging is authorized, launch `software-development/merge-change` with the verified branch or commit as `context.request.source_ref` and the project's validation command; verify the resulting commit and any conflict resolution or post-merge validation. Push only when explicitly authorized. If the objective ends at a reviewed branch or pull request, deliver that instead. This playbook grants no delivery authority beyond the user's request.
6. Close the mission with a disposition ledger mapping every in-scope audit finding to its outcome (fixed with commit and proof, already resolved, rejected with evidence, or deferred pending a stated decision) plus any remaining limits.

These are guidelines, not a script. Re-check findings whenever the target branch moves under parallel work, and keep each batch small enough for an honest, thorough review. Before accepting a run's claims or choosing the next step, read its record, commits, and artifacts directly; consult logs when needed. Keep independent review separate from authorship, resist scope creep beyond the audit findings, and do not count an exhausted review budget or missing response as approval. Ask the user only for genuine product or authority decisions; an earlier authorization remains valid. Keep the audit trail in the mission and its runs rather than creating duplicate reports.
